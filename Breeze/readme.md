## 1. Project summary

**Origin.** A personal experience: after riding a motorbike, the body feels hot and hair is flattened. The discomfort carries into public space even after the ride is over.

**Field observation.** Both entrances of Stasiun Sudimara, 2–3 October 2026, 11.00–14.00 and 16.00–18.00. People who had just got off motorbikes, women and men, fixed their hair and clothes or touched their heads *while still walking*. Observed conclusions:
- the need for thermal relief happens while people are moving, and they deal with it spontaneously and quickly;
- the need covers more than the head.

**Design evolution.**
- It started as a private pod and became a public thermal-comfort intervention.
- Breeze points first sat overhead, then were spread to several points to cover the whole body.

**Current implementation.** A tunnel-like structure filled with randomly placed 3-blade plastic fans. It runs along the route from the paid gate (tap-in) to the train platform.

**Exhibition version.** Part of the module is installed at adjusted scale, with fans spread non-linearly along the visitor route. Visitors move closer, move away, reposition, or adjust their path in response to the airflow.

**Brief requirements to answer** (from the KAK):
- local scale, maximum 50 m²;
- must *measurably* ("terukur") improve safety, comfort or accessibility for women, children and older people;
- documentation of real conditions;
- inclusive "untuk semua" (for everyone) while allowed to focus on one group.

## 2. Critique of the current proposal

### Strengths
- A genuine personal origin, checked by real fieldwork with documented dates and times.
- The shift from private pod to public intervention follows the observed movement pattern.
- Fits the brief's typology (Halte / Fasilitas Transportasi Publik) and the 50 m² limit.

### Gaps a curator will notice

| # | Gap | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No measurements.** The observation says people *cenderung* (tend to) do something | The brief explicitly asks for "terukur" | Count, time, and measure temperatures before and after (Sections 7–10) |
| 2 | **The women's angle was weakened** by the observation that men do it too | "Why is this a women's issue?" needs an argument backed by data | Reframe around *adaptive control*, hijab microclimate, and data split by gender and head covering (Section 4) |
| 3 | **Fan positions are random and not derived** | "Why are the fans *there*?" is otherwise answered with intuition | Weighted-random placement from a data field (Section 11) |
| 4 | **"Aman" (safety) is barely addressed** | It is the exhibition's theme | Safetipin parameters as design checklist and as measurement; the pause-pocket argument (Sections 5–6) |
| 5 | **An unresolved tension:** a fan blows hot air at 32–34 °C, and the obvious fix (misting) would ruin hair and make-up | Honesty, and a test opportunity | Say it openly; test airflow alone versus other options and measure |

## 3. Conceptual framing

### 3.1 Thermal alliesthesia (de Dear; Zhang & Arens, UC Berkeley)
A cooling stimulus feels *pleasant*, not just neutral, when the body is already overheated, which is exactly the moment after getting off a motorbike. The same research tradition finds the head, face and neck are the most sensitive regions for local cooling. This supports placing breeze points at specific body zones rather than cooling the whole space.

### 3.2 Adaptive opportunity / personal control (Karjalainen 2012)
Women, on average, have a **greater need for individual temperature control and adaptive actions** than men. "If females are satisfied, it is highly probable that males are also satisfied." Designing for women therefore also serves everyone, which fits the "untuk semua" requirement.

### 3.3 Proposed positioning statement
> *Breeze Point isn't about giving women more wind. It's about giving them control over when, where and how much air they get: the adaptive opportunity that thermal-comfort research says women need more.*

Read this way, scattered fans become **a range of choices** (intensity, height, position) rather than decoration.

### 3.4 Research question (draft)
> *Di bagian tubuh mana, dan berapa lama, perempuan yang baru turun dari motor membutuhkan aliran udara agar merasa "siap" masuk ke ruang publik — dan bagaimana titik angin dapat menjadi ruang jeda yang aman?*
>
> (On which parts of the body, and for how long, does a woman just off a motorbike need airflow to feel "ready" to enter public space, and how can breeze points become safe places to pause?)

### 3.5 The Dolanan lesson
Honggare & Evanindya's *Makan Kerupuk* paper pairs **one theory** (Huizinga's magic circle) with **one very simple experiment** (game kits, manually timed videos), and gets meaningful conclusions from it. Breeze Point can follow the same structure: **alliesthesia / adaptive control + a simple measured experiment**.

## 4. Evidence: women and thermal comfort

There is **no study showing directly that women "need more breeze."** The argument is built from three strands.

### 4.1 Physiology: how heat is lost
- Compared with men, women's heat loss depends **more on skin blood flow (vasodilation) than on sweating**, regardless of menstrual phase (Inoue et al., 2005).
- Women have a **lower sweat rate** than men of equal fitness, size and acclimation (Penn State review).
- **Caveat:** when fitness, body size and acclimation are standardized, the differences tend to disappear. A later study found that smaller bodies of *either* sex rely more on blood flow than on sweating (Notley et al.).
- **Safe framing:** "On average, women's smaller bodies and lower sweat rates mean heat leaving through the skin matters more, and moving air helps that." Present this as a population tendency, not a fixed biological rule.

### 4.2 Comfort studies: the strongest argument
- Karjalainen (2012, *Indoor Air*; 49 studies): significant gender differences; women express more dissatisfaction, have a greater need for personal control, and should be the primary test subjects.
- Other reviews: women have a **narrower comfort zone** and are **more sensitive to temperature changes**.
- Kingma & van Marken Lichtenbelt (2015, *Nature Climate Change*): comfort standards were based on a "standard man's" metabolic rate.
- Biswas et al. (2024): women's nose temperature was significantly lower than men's and correlated with their thermal sensation votes.
- Note: most of this research is indoors and often about feeling *cold*. Use it to support **control and sensitivity**, not "women feel hotter."

### 4.3 Hijab: directly relevant to Jakarta
- Salsabila, Sakina & Lee (2026, *Fashion and Textiles*), hot-dry climate chamber: microclimate temperature at the **top of the head peaked at around 71–73 °C** for hijab, niqab and burqa, with no significant difference between them. *Hot-dry chamber, not Jakarta conditions; cite carefully.*
- Salsabila et al. (2024), warm-humid condition (30 °C / 70% RH, closer to Jakarta): a **polyester hijab** raised sweat rate and heart rate and was rated more uncomfortable than rayon.
- **Design implication:** for women wearing hijab the scalp is unreachable, so airflow should target the **face, neck and the hijab's edge**.
- **Research gap you can fill:** nobody has studied a hijab worn under a motorbike helmet.

### 4.4 Data to collect yourselves
Split every observation by **woman / man**, **hijab / no hijab**, and **rider / passenger**. If women take longer or touch more body zones, the women's argument becomes data-backed rather than asserted.

## 6. Safety review of the tunnel design

### Risks of an enclosed tunnel between gate and platform
1. **Less visibility.** Enclosure blocks sightlines from staff and other passengers.
2. **Crowding.** Narrowing the route recreates the crush where harassment happens, and fans give people a reason to slow down inside the bottleneck.
3. **Noise.** Fan noise can drown out a call for help.
4. **Fan blades.** 3-blade plastic fans near hair and hijab can catch them, and sit at finger height for children. Use guards, or ducted / slot nozzles.
5. **Accessibility.** Wheelchair width, an unobstructed path for blind passengers (nothing protruding at head height), and emergency evacuation at rush hour.
6. **Permission.** The paid area belongs to KAI, so installation and filming need approval.

### Design responses
| Safetipin parameter | Design move |
|---|---|
| Openness / Visibility | Perforated or slatted skin (canopy rather than enclosure); maximum solidity of about 40%; continuous sightline from gate → platform → staff post |
| Lighting | A light built into each fan module, so pause points are the brightest places |
| Crowd / Walkpath | Breeze points in **pause pockets** beside the main flow; the main path stays clear and is never narrower than it is now |
| Security / Feeling | Pockets placed within view of staff and CCTV |

**Safety argument:** a breeze point creates a **legitimate, visible, well-lit place to stop**. Comfort and safety reinforce each other.

## 7. Method A — Thermal + safety walk (route mapping)

Based on the "thermal walk" method (Vasilikou & Nikolopoulou), the Barcelona citizen-science walks (low-cost sensors), and NUS Singapore's study of transitions between thermal environments (tropical climate).

1. Divide the route from gate to platform into points every 2–3 m.
2. At each point record:
   - air temperature, humidity and wind speed (ESP32 + DHT22 + cheap anemometer);
   - **thermal sensation vote**, −3 (cold) to +3 (hot);
   - **safety score**, 0–3 for each Safetipin parameter.
3. Participants: 10–20, mostly women who arrived by motorbike, including hijab wearers.
4. Output: a **profile along the route** of discomfort and perceived safety, which becomes the input for Section 11.
5. A before/after perception survey (as in Despacio's Bucaramanga tactical-urbanism evaluation) meets the brief's *terukur* requirement.

## 8. Method B — Same pipeline as the gado-gado project (for reference only)

*The earlier workflow: photograph gado-gado carts → label manually with YOLO in Google Colab → measure panels with OpenCV → averages (for example 7 horizontal × 2 vertical) → voxel guide.*

Applied to Sudimara (video, YOLO person class + tracking):

| Data | Extraction | Design parameter |
|---|---|---|
| Walking speed | Box centre tracked across frames, pixels → metres via floor tiles | Tunnel length (L = v × t) |
| Stop location and time | Tracks stationary for more than ~3 s → heatmap | Pause-pocket positions |
| Flow and crowding | People per minute; width of the walking line at peak | Clear path width |
| Body height range | Calibrated box height, 5th–95th percentile | Vertical band for outlets |
| Head covering | Custom labels: hijab / helmet / none | Share of face/neck outlets versus top-of-head |
| Fixing-up action (optional) | Custom box class "hand at head" | Where the first fan starts |
| Distance between people | Distance between box centres | Pause-pocket size |
| Sun and shade | Floor-area brightness over time | Zones that need breeze most |

**Not chosen as the main method:** it is too similar to the previous project, and video raises privacy and permission problems. It remains a fallback.

## 9. Method C (recommended) — Growing the form during the exhibition

Inspired by the **Elytra Filament Pavilion** (V&A 2016). Its canopy grew over the season in response to occupancy data from anonymous thermal imaging cameras, overlaid with real-time UTCI thermal-comfort maps.

### How it differs from the previous project
| | Gado-gado (before) | Breeze Point (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| Data | Photos, collected once | Sensor time series, continuous |
| Processing | Averages | A weekly feedback loop |
| Output | One fixed voxel | A form that changes from week 1 to week 3 |
| Role of the public | Object being measured | Co-author |

### Process
1. **State 0 (opening, 1 December):** a minimal frame with a few fans, placed using the Sudimara observations.
2. **Sensing (continuous):**
   - low-resolution thermal arrays → occupancy and dwell heatmap (anonymous);
   - a distance sensor at each fan → how long each module is used;
   - a "Panas di sini!" (I'm hot here!) button or physical tokens → what visitors report;
   - one thermal-camera station → each person's **facial temperature on arrival and after N seconds of breeze**.
3. **Weekly rule:**
   - cells with high use × high reported heat → **add** a fan module;
   - fans rarely used → **move** them;
   - empty areas → **open** the skin (visibility).
4. **Final state (22 December):** the structure is the record of how bodies responded. Display it with photos of the intermediate states and the heatmaps.
5. **Feedback** into the real Sudimara design.

**Measurable outcome:** the temperature drop before versus after the breeze is the "terukur" evidence.

**Curator check:** growing during the exhibition needs permission and spare modules. **Fallback:** log the data for three weeks and present a "final form generated from visitor data" render or model at closing.

## 10. Sensing tools that replace videotaping

| Tool | Captures | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-resolution thermal array** (AMG8833 8×8; MLX90640 32×24) on ESP32, about Rp 300k–1jt | Presence, count, location, dwell | No faces. Counting can run entirely on the ESP32 with no images stored (Papanashi et al., 2026). Even 4×2 pixels detect reliably at ≥4 fps (Rinta-Homi et al., 2021) |
| **Phone thermal camera** (InfiRay / FLIR One) | Facial skin temperature | Ears, nose and cheeks estimate comfort with about 85% accuracy (Li et al., 2018). Using *temperature differences* cancels calibration drift; 83–96% accuracy, partly measured outdoors (He et al., 2022). Keep the camera within about 1 m (Zheng et al., 2023). Use **relative change**, not absolute values |
| **Time-of-flight / ultrasonic distance sensor** per fan | Presence in front of the fan, duration | Gives usage data for each module |
| **Push button / physical tokens** | Self-reported heat or comfort | Participatory, like Kimbell's *Physical Bar Charts* |
| **Body-map sticker wall** | Where visitors feel hot after commuting | Analogue heatmap built up over 3 weeks |
| **Portable microclimate kit** (ESP32 + DHT22 + anemometer) | Temperature, humidity, wind along the route | For the thermal walk (Method A) |

## 11. From data to form: synthesis rules

The core deliverable is one table: **data → parameter → geometry**. It answers "why are the fans there?"

### 11.1 Bounding dimensions
- **Length:** L = speed while fixing up × fixing-up time (placeholder: 0.8 m/s × 10 s ≈ 8 m; replace with measured values). This also justifies the 2700 mm dimension.
- **Clear path width:** measured peak walking line plus margin.
- **Outlet height band:** face-to-shoulder range across body-height percentiles.

### 11.2 Voxel / grid logic (Grasshopper)
1. Divide the tunnel volume into cells (for example 30 × 30 × 30 cm).
2. **Remove** cells inside the clear path (flow and safety rule).
3. Cells outside the height band → structure or empty only, no fans.
4. **Score** the remaining cells, for example:
   `score = dwell/occupancy × reported heat (or sun exposure) × head-covering factor`
   where the head-covering factor raises face/neck-height cells where hijab is common.
5. **Weighted-random placement** of fans from the score field: *random, but not arbitrary*. It still looks scattered, and every position can be traced to data.
6. **Skin rule:** solidity ≤ ~40% so there is always visibility.

### 11.3 For Method C
Re-run steps 4–6 each week with the accumulated sensor data. Each run produces the next physical state.

## 12. Precedents

### Methodological framework
- **Jeroen van Ameijde (AA → CUHK).** Data-driven generative design from observed human behaviour, using a four-stage process: **gather → analyse → translate → implement**.
  - *Data-driven Urban Design* (SPOOL, 2022): the framework, in the spirit of Negroponte's Architecture Machine.
  - *Generative Architectural Design and Build Strategies based on the Mapping of Human Behaviour* (ISARC 2018): small projects with low-cost fabrication plus generative algorithms; cites Whyte; discusses ethical safeguards.
  - *The Architecture Machine Revisited* (SPOOL, 2019): AA experiments 2011–2017.
  - *Data-Driven Urban Porosity* (CAADRIA 2018, on CumInCAD).
  - *Decoding micro-social interactions in public space* (Computational Urban Science, 2025): computer-vision behaviour analysis.

### Live and sensor data → form
- **Elytra Filament Pavilion** (Menges, Knippers, Transsolar; V&A 2016): thermal-camera occupancy + UTCI comfort maps → the canopy grows. **Main precedent.**
- **Nam, *Programming Intelligent Architecture to be Responsive to Real-Time Data*** (CAADRIA 2019, on CumInCAD): urban open data (social activity, weather) mapped to architectural behaviour.
- **Frogheri et al. (2017):** an Arduino-based sensitive pavilion that changes colour and moves in response to live context data.
- **Tapias & Schmitt (ETH, 2014):** outdoor thermal comfort as an indicator in a generative urban-form tool.
- **He et al. (2025):** Gehl's principles retested with webcam data and computer vision. People stand where visual connectivity is good; a good place to stay must be easy to walk through.

### Observation and evaluation of public life
- **William H. Whyte, Street Life Project / *The Social Life of Small Urban Spaces*:** time-lapse observation; later reanalysed with AI, which found people today walk about 15% faster and linger less.
- **Benchmark NSW (MIT LCAU + UNSW):** a DIY, privacy-preserving computer-vision kit (GoPro + Jetson Nano), co-designed with a focus on women, girls and gender-diverse people's perceived safety. Strong precedent for women + safety + temporary furniture.
- **Despacio, Bucaramanga:** before/after perception surveys of a tactical-urbanism intervention (79% felt unsafe crossing before → 71% felt safe or very safe after).
- **Safetipin:** a participatory safety-audit app, including a 2016 Jakarta report.

### Participatory data physicalization
- **Lucy Kimbell, *Physical Bar Charts*:** visitors take coloured badges; the dropping levels form a live bar chart.
- **Knitting Interactive Spaces (2024):** a circular knitting machine physicalizes gallery visitors over a year.
- **Harassment Plants (2018):** a situated physicalization of women's harassment stories at a Brazilian lakeside.

### Bartlett Unit 21 (related, less direct)
- **Simon Felix Rosbach, *Choreomata* (2025):** motion tracking + LiDAR → movement catalogue → rearranged church elements.
- **Jeffrey Cheung, *Parque Para Perros Español* (2024):** Kinect for the human, Python colour-marker tracking for the dog → design tools.

### Reference projects from the brief
- **My house is ill** (Architecture for Dialogue, Khoj 2022): air-quality sensing → making invisible air conditions visible.
- **Dolanan, Makan Kerupuk** (Honggare & Evanindya, ARSNET 2021): a simple experiment that produced meaningful data.

## 13. Ethics & practical notes
- **Permissions:** Stasiun Sudimara is KAI property, so ask before filming or installing sensors.
- **Privacy:** prefer thermal arrays (no faces). If video is used: blur faces, keep only coordinates, delete footage. State this in the method section, following van Ameijde's point on safeguards.
- **Calibration:** pixels-to-metres reference on day one; for the thermal camera, use relative change before vs after.
- **Labeling:** label *visible* attributes (hijab / helmet / none), not inferred gender.
- **Sample:** 10–20 thermal-walk participants; a few hundred labeled frames if video is used.
- **Physical safety:** guarded fans, nothing at head height in the path, emergency width kept clear.
